CivicAlign · Pillars 4–6

Methodology for every number

Each number on the page has one entry here: where it comes from, what is done to the source, how it is calculated, which recorded versions it depends on, and what it does not tell you. The page cannot show a number that has no entry.

Rules that apply to every number

  • Every calculation uses Voteview’s nominate_dim1 in its own units (−1 to +1). The main page shows it as a display position, (score + 1) × 50, on a line from 0 (liberal side) to 100 (conservative side): a position only, not a score, with the raw value always available underneath (see “How the page presents these numbers”).
  • State public estimates stay on the American Ideology Project’s own survey scale. They are never placed on the senator scale without a validated bridge, and none exists, so no senator-to-state distance is calculated.
  • A national public centre has not been defined, so no Senate-to-public or committee-to-public gap is calculated.
  • The population-weighted average is the main Pillar 5 comparison. Medians are a secondary comparison, shown in details only. Both methods are candidates, not settled definitions.
  • Committee membership dates are the dates CivicAlign observed them, not official appointment dates.
  • Numbers describe voting records, seats and populations. They are not judgments of any person, and they say nothing about why a bill passed or failed.
